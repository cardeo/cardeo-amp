# Vision

Cardeo Amp is a local-first music player for people who still own their music.
It exists to make local music files feel immediate, durable, and personal again.

The project starts from a simple belief: a music player does not need accounts,
subscriptions, cloud services, recommendation algorithms, or social features to
be useful. It needs to play the music the user chooses, quickly and reliably,
with enough personality to feel like a real piece of software rather than a
generic media surface.

## Purpose

Cardeo Amp should provide a focused way to listen to local music collections.
Those collections may include purchased albums, ripped CDs, Bandcamp downloads,
personal recordings, demos, live sets, archived mixes, or any other files the
listener keeps on their own machine.

The app should respect those files as the user's library, not as content waiting
to be uploaded, analyzed, ranked, or monetized.

## Design Philosophy

Cardeo Amp should be lightweight, direct, and expressive.

The interface should prioritize core listening workflows: choosing files,
building a queue or playlist, controlling playback, seeing progress, and
adjusting volume. Extra features should earn their place by making local
listening better, not by copying streaming platforms.

The app can have character. It can feel tactile, themed, and a little playful.
That personality should support clarity rather than compete with it.

## Local-First Principles

- The user's files stay local.
- Playback should not require an account.
- Playback should not require an internet connection.
- The app should not depend on a remote catalog.
- The user should decide what to play.
- Local data should be understandable and portable where possible.

Local-first does not mean refusing every future integration. It means the core
experience must remain useful when the network is gone.

## Ownership Over Access

Cardeo Amp favors ownership over access. Streaming platforms are convenient, but
they also make music feel temporary. Albums disappear, licenses change, services
shut down, and user accounts become points of failure.

Owned files are different. They can be backed up, moved, renamed, tagged,
archived, and played decades later. Cardeo Amp should serve that kind of
relationship with music.

## Software With Personality

The project is inspired by the era when small music players felt memorable and
distinct. Cardeo Amp should carry that spirit forward while remaining original
in name, visuals, interaction design, and branding.

The goal is not nostalgia for its own sake. The goal is software that feels
personal, fast, and made by people who care about the listening experience.

## Customization And Themes

Themes should eventually let users change the visual character of the player
without changing how playback works. A theme may alter colors, textures,
spacing, typography, control styling, and visual mood. It should not redefine
what play, pause, seek, shuffle, or repeat mean.

This separation matters because themeability should make the app more personal
without making it fragile.

## Open Source Philosophy

Cardeo Amp should be open for people to inspect, modify, learn from, and extend.
The project should prefer readable code, plain documentation, and small
decisions that are easy to revisit.

The community should be able to help shape the player while preserving the core
identity: local music, user ownership, speed, simplicity, and personality.
